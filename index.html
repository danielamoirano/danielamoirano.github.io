Daniela Moirano

PhD Candidate & Research Associate, Chair of Sport and Health Management,
Technical University of Munich (TUM)

I study the psychological and contextual drivers of environmentally responsible and reduced consumption: why people consume, and how they can be encouraged to consume less and more sustainably. My work focuses on food: sustainable and healthy diets, meat reduction and alternative proteins, tested with experiments, surveys, choice analysis and in-depth interviews. Going forward, I want to work increasingly with behavioural data, capturing what people actually buy and eat, and to explore how AI and large language models can advance psychological research on consumption.

Mission & Vision

Mission

My mission is to understand what drives people to consume more sustainably and to consume less, and to turn these insights into interventions that work in everyday life. I combine theories from social and consumer psychology with rigorous experimental methods. I want to rely increasingly on behavioural data, capturing what people actually do rather than what they say they intend to do, and to use AI and large language models as tools for psychological research, especially in the food domain.

Vision

My vision is a world in which sustainable consumption is the easy, normal and enjoyable choice for everyone, contributing to UN Sustainable Development Goal 12: responsible consumption and production.

Research

I am completing my PhD in economics and social sciences (Dr. rer. pol.) at the Technical University of Munich, focusing on consumer psychology. My dissertation, Explaining the Adoption of Sustainable Consumption Practices, examines what drives people to consume more sustainably and to consume less.

01

Sustainable Food Consumption

What drives people to adopt diets that are good for both the planet and their health? Extending the Comprehensive Action Determination Model, I compare the roles of environmental concern, social and personal norms, perceived consumer effectiveness, self-efficacy and health consciousness among German consumers.

As a visiting researcher with the Low Harm Hedonism Initiative at the University of Queensland, I also studied how omnivores perceive blended meat products (part meat, part plants) through in-depth interviews. Follow-up work tests menu-label disclosure experimentally and in a tasting study with UQ's FoodLab. This builds on my master's research on plant-based, insect-based and cultured meat.

Survey research · Structural equation modelling · Qualitative interviews · Online & tasting experiments · Food Quality and Preference (2025)

02

Social Psychology

When people compare themselves to someone who consumes less, are they more willing to refrain from purchases? I test this experimentally, examining green consumption values and temperance character strengths as moderators, including in the context of Christmas gift-giving in the UK.

Experiments · Moderation & mediation · Social comparison theory

03

Food & Wellbeing

Resilient by Recipe (with Karina Konieczny) explores whether a dietary lunch intervention can support entrepreneurs' mental health, combining founder interviews with wearable-based physiological measurement. The intervention study is planned for 2027.

Field intervention · Wearables · Validated psychological scales

Next

Where I Am Heading: Real Behaviour, AI and Psychology

In my postdoc, I want to build on this work in two directions. First, I want to study what people actually do rather than what they report: purchase and consumption data, field and behavioural experiments with real choices, and data from wearables. Second, I want to explore how AI and large language models can support psychological research, for example in designing and pretesting interventions, analysing open text and interview data, or modelling consumer responses, and how AI-based tools can themselves encourage more sustainable choices.

Behavioural data · Field experiments · AI & large language models · Consumer psychology

Methods & Expertise

My work is primarily quantitative and experimental. I received doctoral training in experimental methods in consumer research (VHB ProDok), experimental research design (GSERM), experimental economics and applied choice analysis.

Experimental Design

  • Online, lab and field experiments
  • Between-subjects manipulations (social comparison, social norms)
  • Discrete choice experiments
  • A priori power analysis

Data Collection

  • Qualtrics and Sawtooth
  • Online panels (Prolific)
  • Behavioural choice measures
  • Wearable data

Analysis

  • Structural equation modelling
  • Moderation and mediation
  • ANCOVA, regression, choice models
  • R, Mplus, SPSS, Stata (MAXQDA for interviews)
